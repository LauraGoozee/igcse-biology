Breathing and Exercise Lab

Compare the carbon dioxide in inhaled and exhaled air, then find out how exercise changes breathing rate.

Carbon dioxide in inhaled and exhaled air

Limewater turns cloudy (milky) with carbon dioxide. When you breathe in, air is pulled through tube A. When you breathe out, your breath bubbles through tube B.

clear = little CO₂
cloudy (milky) = lots of CO₂
Breaths: 0

Why? Inhaled air has about 0.04% carbon dioxide. Exhaled air has about 4%, because your cells release carbon dioxide in respiration.

Results

Tube B goes cloudy after far fewer breaths, so exhaled air contains more carbon dioxide.

Why does breathing rate go up? Muscles respire faster during exercise. They need more oxygen and make more carbon dioxide. Breathing faster and deeper takes in more oxygen and removes the extra carbon dioxide.
