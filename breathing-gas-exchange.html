Breathing and Gas Exchange

See how the ribs, intercostal muscles and diaphragm make us breathe, try the bell jar model, and watch gases diffuse in an alveolus.

Breathing in and breathing out

Muscles change the volume of the thorax (chest). This changes the pressure, and air moves from high pressure to low pressure.

Labels

What happens, in order

Breathing inBreathing out
1The (external) intercostal muscles contract. The ribs move up and out.The (external) intercostal muscles relax. The ribs move down and in.
2The diaphragm contracts. It flattens and moves down.The diaphragm relaxes. It goes back to a dome shape (moves up).
3The volume of the thorax increases.The volume of the thorax decreases.
4The pressure inside decreases (lower than the air outside).The pressure inside increases (higher than the air outside).
5Air moves in, down the pressure gradient.Air moves out, down the pressure gradient.

Steps 1 and 2 happen at the same time. Exam tip: say "the diaphragm contracts and flattens", not "the diaphragm moves down" on its own. Do not say the internal intercostals contract when breathing in.

Extra: when you breathe out hard (forced breathing out, e.g. in exercise), the internal intercostal muscles contract to pull the ribs further down and in. In normal, quiet breathing out, the internal intercostals do not need to contract.

Limitations: how is the model different?

  • The jar cannot move, but the ribs move up and out. There are no intercostal muscles.
  • The rubber sheet is pulled down by a hand. The diaphragm contracts and flattens by itself; when it relaxes it is dome-shaped.
  • Balloons are hollow. Real lungs are made of many bronchioles and alveoli.
  • There is no gas exchange in the model.

Gas exchange in an alveolus

Oxygen diffuses from the air in the alveolus into the blood. Carbon dioxide diffuses from the blood into the alveolus.

How are alveoli adapted for gas exchange?

many alveolia very large surface area for diffusion
walls one cell thick (alveolus and capillary)a short diffusion distance
good blood supply (many capillaries)blood carries oxygen away and brings carbon dioxide, so a steep concentration gradient is kept
ventilation (breathing)brings fresh air in and removes carbon dioxide, so the concentration gradient is kept
moist lininggases dissolve before they diffuse

Try turning breathing or blood flow off: the concentration gradient gets smaller, so diffusion slows down.
