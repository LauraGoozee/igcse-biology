Diffusion and Osmosis

See how particles spread out, how water moves through a partially permeable membrane, and what happens to cells in different solutions.

Diffusion

Particles move about randomly. They spread out until they are evenly spread. The net movement is from a high concentration to a low concentration.

Surface area to volume ratio

Cubes of pink agar are put into acid. The acid diffuses in from every face and the pink colour disappears. Which cube goes colourless all the way through first?

What does this mean?

  • The bigger the cube, the smaller its surface area to volume ratio.
  • In a big cube, the middle is a long distance from the surface, so substances take longer to get there.
  • Small organisms (like single cells) have a large SA : volume ratio, so diffusion through their surface is enough.
  • Large organisms have a small SA : volume ratio, so they need exchange surfaces (like the lungs and villi) and a transport system (like the blood).

Osmosis

The membrane is partially permeable: small water molecules can pass through, but large sugar molecules cannot. Water moves from the dilute side to the more concentrated side.

Key words

osmosisthe net movement of water from a dilute solution to a more concentrated solution through a partially permeable membrane
partially permeable membranelets small molecules (like water) through, but not large ones (like sugar)
dilute solutiona lot of water and a little solute (sugar)
concentrated solutionless water and more solute
passivediffusion and osmosis do not need energy from respiration

Watch the water levels: the level rises on the side with more sugar. When both sides have the same concentration, water still moves both ways but there is no net movement.
