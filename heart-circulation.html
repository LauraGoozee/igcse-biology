How the heart pumps blood

The heart is a double pump. The right side pumps deoxygenated blood to the lungs. The left side pumps oxygenated blood to the rest of the body.

The diagram is drawn as if you are looking at the person, so the left side of the heart is on the right of the screen. The animation is slowed down.

Key facts

valvesstop blood flowing backwards (prevent backflow). Valves are not muscles: they are pushed open and shut by the pressure of the blood.
left ventriclehas the thickest muscle wall. It contracts with more force to pump blood at high pressure all around the body.
right ventriclehas a thinner wall. It pumps blood only to the lungs, at lower pressure, so the lung capillaries are not damaged.
septumthe wall between the left and right sides. It keeps oxygenated and deoxygenated blood apart.
coronary arteriessupply the heart muscle with oxygen and glucose for respiration

Follow a red blood cell

Blood goes through the heart twice on each trip around the body: heart → lungs → heart → body → heart. This is a double circulation.

    Heart rate: exercise and adrenaline

    Press a button and watch what happens to the heart rate.

    Why does the heart rate change?

    during exerciseMuscles respire faster to release more energy for contraction. They need more oxygen and glucose and must remove more carbon dioxide. The heart beats faster (and pumps more blood each beat) to deliver blood more quickly.
    after exerciseThe heart rate stays high for a while. Extra oxygen is needed to break down the lactic acid made by anaerobic respiration (the oxygen debt).
    adrenalineA hormone made by the adrenal glands ("fight or flight"). It travels in the blood and makes the heart beat faster, preparing the body for action.
