Beetroot Permeability Lab

Investigate how temperature and ethanol affect the permeability of cell membranes, by measuring how much red pigment leaks out of beetroot cells.

Spec 2.3 Core Practical 3Links to 2.2 membrane structure
1

The method

Beetroot cells store a red pigment, betalain, in their vacuoles. The pigment can only leak out if the membranes around it are damaged. Read each step, then open “Why?” to check your reasoning.

1

Use a cork borer to cut cylinders of beetroot, then cut them all to the same length.

Why?

So every piece has the same surface area and volume. A bigger piece would release more pigment whatever the treatment.

2

Rinse the pieces in distilled water until the water stays clear.

Why?

Cutting breaks open some cells. Rinsing removes the pigment they released, so only pigment that leaks during the experiment is measured.

3

Place one piece in each tube containing the same volume of distilled water, in water baths at a range of temperatures. Leave them all for the same time, for example 20 minutes. For the ethanol investigation, use a range of ethanol concentrations at one temperature instead.

Why?

Volume and time are control variables. More water would dilute the pigment, and a longer time would let more pigment leak out. Let the water reach the bath temperature before adding the beetroot.

4

Remove the beetroot and shake each tube gently to mix the liquid.

Why?

The pigment needs to be evenly spread through the liquid before a sample is taken.

5

Set the colorimeter to a blue-green filter and zero it using a cuvette of distilled water.

Why?

A red solution absorbs blue-green light most strongly, so this filter gives the most sensitive readings. Zeroing with distilled water means any absorbance measured is due to the pigment only.

6

Measure the absorbance of a sample of liquid from each tube. Repeat each condition three times.

Why?

Higher absorbance means more pigment in the water, so the membranes were more permeable. Repeats let you spot anomalies and calculate a mean.

Safety: beetroot juice stains skin and clothes. Take care with hot water baths and sharp cork borers. Ethanol is highly flammable, so keep it away from naked flames.

Practice questions

Written for practice on this core practical, with suggested marking points.

Explain the shape of the graph for the effect of temperature on the absorbance of the solution.

(4)
Show marking points
  • below about 40 °C, absorbance is low and changes little, because the membranes are intact
  • as temperature increases, phospholipids gain kinetic energy and move more, so the bilayer becomes more permeable
  • above about 45 °C, membrane proteins denature, leaving gaps in the membrane
  • more pigment leaks out, so absorbance increases steeply
  • at high temperatures absorbance levels off because almost all the pigment has leaked out

Explain why the beetroot pieces were rinsed before the investigation.

(2)
Show marking points
  • to remove pigment released from cells damaged when the beetroot was cut
  • so that the absorbance only measures pigment that leaked during the investigation / otherwise absorbance would be too high

Explain why the colorimeter is zeroed using distilled water.

(2)
Show marking points
  • to calibrate the colorimeter / set a baseline of zero absorbance
  • so that the absorbance measured is due only to the pigment (not the water or the cuvette)

Suggest why increasing the concentration of ethanol increases the permeability of the membrane.

(2)
Show marking points
  • ethanol is a solvent that dissolves lipids / disrupts the phospholipid bilayer
  • ethanol denatures membrane proteins
  • so gaps form in the membrane and pigment can leak out

Give two variables that should be controlled in the temperature investigation, and how.

(2)
Show marking points
  • size of beetroot pieces: same cork borer, cut to the same length
  • volume of water: measured with a pipette or measuring cylinder
  • time in the water bath: timed with a stopwatch
  • source of beetroot: all pieces from the same beetroot

Absorbance data are simulated from a model, with random variation and occasional anomalies. Real values depend on the beetroot, the time and the colorimeter.
