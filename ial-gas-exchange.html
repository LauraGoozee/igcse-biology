    Inside an alveolar sac

    Each airway ends in a cluster of alveoli, separated by thin walls. Oxygen diffuses from the air into the blood, and carbon dioxide diffuses out. Ventilation and blood flow keep the concentration gradients steep. Try the scenarios, or change the sliders yourself.

    3

    Fick’s law with real numbers

    Rate of diffusion ∝ surface area × difference in concentration ÷ thickness of the surface. The starting values are typical for healthy human lungs at rest.

    Healthy adult lungs: roughly 50–100 m², from hundreds of millions of alveoli.
    Alveolar air about 13.3 kPa; blood arriving in the capillaries about 5.3 kPa.
    Alveolar wall plus capillary wall: typically less than 1 µm.

    Exam tip: Fick’s law gives a rate that is proportional to these factors, so compare values as ratios. Halving surface area halves the rate; halving the thickness doubles it.
