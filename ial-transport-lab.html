Surface area to volume ratio: agar cubes

Cubes of agar containing phenolphthalein and sodium hydroxide are pink. They are placed in hydrochloric acid. As acid diffuses in and neutralises the alkali, the agar turns colourless from the outside in.

Length of side / cmSurface area / cm²Volume / cm³Surface area : volumeVolume turned colourless / %Time to turn fully colourless / min

As a cube gets bigger, its volume increases faster than its surface area, so SA:V falls. The centre of a large cube is further from the surface, so it takes much longer for substances to diffuse there. This is why large organisms need specialised exchange surfaces such as alveoli.

In this model the depth reached by the acid increases with the square root of time. Real times depend on acid concentration and temperature.

3

Facilitated diffusion and active transport

Both use membrane proteins. Only active transport can move a substance against its concentration gradient, and it needs ATP from respiration.

Watch it happen in the membrane

Active transport carrier, current step

Watch the counts. Ions through the channel and glucose through the carrier even out, because facilitated diffusion only gives net movement down the concentration gradient. The active transport carrier keeps moving its ions into the cell, where they are already more concentrated, using one ATP each time.

Rate of uptake and concentration

Facilitated diffusion levels off because every carrier or channel protein is in use. You can see this in the membrane above: the carrier can only move one glucose molecule at a time. Adding more carriers raises the plateau. Simple diffusion through the bilayer keeps increasing with concentration.

Concentration inside a cell over time

Facilitated diffusion stops when the concentration inside equals the outside. Active transport keeps going above it. A respiratory inhibitor stops ATP production, so active uptake stops.

4

Endocytosis and exocytosis

Large molecules and particles cannot pass through the membrane or its proteins. They are moved in vesicles instead, which needs ATP.
