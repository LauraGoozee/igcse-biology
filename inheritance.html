Key words
AlleleA different form of a gene
DominantShown in the phenotype even if only one copy is present
RecessiveOnly shown if there are two copies (no dominant allele)
HomozygousTwo identical alleles, e.g. TT or tt
HeterozygousTwo different alleles, e.g. Tt
GenotypeThe alleles an organism has, e.g. Tt
PhenotypeThe characteristic you can see, e.g. tall
Exam tips
  • Choose a letter where the capital and small letter look different, e.g. T and t. Avoid S and s, or C and c.
  • Write the parent genotypes, then the gametes, then the Punnett square.
  • Give the ratio of phenotypes, e.g. 3 tall : 1 short.
  • A ratio is a prediction. Real numbers of offspring are rarely exact, especially when there are only a few.
  • Probability can be written as a fraction, a percentage or a decimal: 1/4 = 25% = 0.25.

Natural selection: the four steps

1 · VariationPale and dark mothsColour is caused by different alleles. The dark allele first came from a mutation.
2 · SurvivalCamouflaged moths surviveBirds see and eat the moths that stand out.
3 · ReproductionSurvivors breedThey pass on their alleles for colour.
4 · Over generationsThe allele becomes more commonThe advantageous colour increases in the population.

In Britain in the 1800s, factory smoke killed the lichen and covered tree bark in soot. Dark moths became much more common in polluted areas. After the air became cleaner, pale moths became common again.

Exam answer · explain natural selection
  1. There is variation in the population, caused by differences in alleles (from mutation).
  2. Individuals with an advantageous characteristic are more likely to survive.
  3. They are more likely to reproduce and pass on their advantageous alleles.
  4. Over many generations, the advantageous allele becomes more common in the population.
Avoid these mistakes
  • Moths do not change colour to match the tree. Each moth's colour is set by its alleles.
  • Moths do not choose or try to be dark. Mutations are random.
  • Selection acts on individuals, but the population changes over generations.
  • Always mention alleles being passed on to offspring.

How resistance spreads

1 · VariationRandom mutationA new allele for resistance appears.
2 · Increased survivalAntibiotic kills the othersBacteria with the resistance allele survive.
3 · Increased reproductionSurvivors reproduceThe resistance allele is passed on.
4 · Allele frequency risesA resistant strainThe antibiotic no longer works. A different one is needed.
Exam answer · how resistance develops
  1. A random mutation gives a bacterium an allele for resistance.
  2. When the antibiotic is used, bacteria without the allele die. Resistant bacteria survive.
  3. The survivors reproduce and pass on the resistance allele.
  4. Over several generations the resistance allele becomes more common, forming a resistant strain.
Avoid these mistakes
  • The antibiotic does not cause the mutation. The mutation is random and happens first.
  • Bacteria do not "get used to" or "become immune to" an antibiotic.
  • Say bacteria become resistant, not the person.
  • Resistant infections are harder to treat, so a different antibiotic is needed.
