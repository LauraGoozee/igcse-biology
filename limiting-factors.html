Photosynthesis: Limiting Factors

Change the light, carbon dioxide and temperature. See which factor is limiting, how the graphs change, and how farmers use this to increase the growth of plants.

Limiting factors and graphs

Photosynthesis needs light, carbon dioxide and a suitable temperature. The factor in the shortest supply holds the rate back: this is the limiting factor.

How to use this

  1. Choose the factor to change. Its slider is highlighted. The other two factors stay the same (control variables).
  2. Press Play, or move the highlighted slider, or drag the dot on the graph. Watch the pondweed, the bars and the graph change together.
  3. Press Keep this line, change one of the other factors, then play again to compare.

Reading the bars

Each bar shows how fast photosynthesis could go with that factor. The rate (dashed line) can only be as high as the lowest bar, the limiting factor.

Reading the graph

line going upthe factor on the x-axis is the limiting factor
line flat (plateau)the x-axis factor is no longer limiting. Another factor is limiting.
higher plateauthe other factor was increased, so the rate can go higher
temperature line fallsabove the optimum, enzymes denature
lightprovides the energy for photosynthesis
carbon dioxidea reactant: it is used to make glucose
temperaturecontrols the enzymes: more kinetic energy and collisions up to the optimum; too hot and they denature

Exam tip: on the flat part, do not write "light is the limiting factor". Write "light is not limiting; CO₂ (or temperature) is limiting".

Increasing the growth of plants

A farmer wants crops to grow faster. Which equipment should they turn on? Each one costs money.

Why use a glasshouse?

  • The glass lets light in and traps heat, so the temperature is higher.
  • Farmers can add artificial light, heating and extra carbon dioxide (e.g. a paraffin burner).
  • This increases the rate of photosynthesis, so crops grow faster and the yield is higher.
  • It is only worth paying for the factor that is limiting. Increasing a factor that is not limiting costs money but does not increase the rate.
