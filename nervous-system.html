Exam answer · describe the reflex arc
  1. Receptors in the skin detect the heat (stimulus).
  2. An electrical impulse travels along a sensory neurone to the spinal cord.
  3. The impulse passes across a synapse to a relay neurone.
  4. The impulse passes to a motor neurone.
  5. The motor neurone carries the impulse to the effector (biceps muscle).
  6. The muscle contracts and the hand is pulled away.
Exam answer · why are reflexes useful?
  1. Reflexes are automatic: you do not think about them.
  2. They are rapid, because the conscious part of the brain is not involved.
  3. This reduces damage to the body (for example, a burn).
  4. You feel the pain after the hand has already moved.

Neurones do not touch each other. There is a tiny gap between them called a synapse. The electrical impulse cannot jump across the gap, so a chemical carries the message.

Watch out

The receptor molecules on neurone 2 are not the same as receptor cells in sense organs. They are proteins that the neurotransmitter binds to.

Only neurone 1 releases neurotransmitter, and only neurone 2 has receptor molecules. This is why the impulse can only travel in one direction.

Exam answer · how an impulse crosses a synapse
  1. An electrical impulse arrives at the end of the neurone.
  2. This causes the release of neurotransmitter (a chemical messenger).
  3. The neurotransmitter diffuses across the synapse.
  4. It binds to receptors on the next neurone.
  5. This starts a new electrical impulse in the next neurone.
Key words
  1. Synapse: the tiny gap between two neurones.
  2. Neurotransmitter: a chemical that carries the message across the gap.
  3. Diffuse: move from a high concentration to a low concentration.
  4. Impulse: an electrical signal that travels along a neurone.

The central nervous system (CNS)
  1. The CNS is the brain and spinal cord.
  2. It is linked to sense organs by nerves.
  3. A nerve is a bundle of neurones.
  4. The CNS coordinates the response.
Three types of neurone
  1. Sensory neurone: carries impulses from receptors to the CNS.
  2. Relay neurone: inside the CNS, links sensory and motor neurones.
  3. Motor neurone: carries impulses from the CNS to effectors.
