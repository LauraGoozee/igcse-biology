Photosynthesis and the Leaf

See how a plant makes glucose using light, carbon dioxide and water, and explore how the leaf is adapted for photosynthesis.

The process of photosynthesis

Plants make their own food. In the chloroplasts, light energy is used to turn carbon dioxide and water into glucose and oxygen. Light energy is changed into chemical energy stored in glucose.

Key facts

where?in the chloroplasts, mostly in the palisade cells of the leaf
chlorophyllthe green pigment in chloroplasts that absorbs light energy
energy changelight energy → chemical energy (stored in glucose)
carbon dioxidediffuses in from the air through the stomata
waterabsorbed by the roots and carried to the leaf in the xylem
oxygena waste product: diffuses out through the stomata

What does the plant do with the glucose?

  • respiration: to release energy
  • changed into starch for storage
  • changed into cellulose to make cell walls
  • changed into sucrose to be transported in the phloem
  • used with nitrate ions to make amino acids and proteins

How is the leaf adapted for photosynthesis?

large surface areaabsorbs more light; more area for CO₂ to diffuse in
thinshort distance for CO₂ to diffuse to the cells
waxy cuticlewaterproof: reduces water loss. Transparent, so light passes through
transparent upper epidermislets light through to the palisade layer
palisade mesophyllnear the top surface; tall cells packed closely; many chloroplasts: absorbs most light
spongy mesophyll with air spacesCO₂ can diffuse to the palisade cells; large surface area for gas exchange
stomata and guard cellslet CO₂ in and O₂ out; guard cells open and close them
vascular bundle (vein)xylem brings water; phloem takes sugars away
